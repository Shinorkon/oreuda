## Facet: Goal-Setting Frameworks & Taxonomy

**Research Date**: July 2025
**Sources Consulted**: 45+ academic papers, behavioral science journals, productivity methodology books, coaching frameworks, and 2024-2025 app innovation reviews
**Searches Performed**: 14 independent search queries

---

### Key Findings

- **Locke & Latham's Goal-Setting Theory** remains the most validated framework: specific, difficult goals outperform "do your best" goals in ~96% of 400+ studies, with an average 17% performance lift [^181^]. Five conditions must all be present: Clarity, Challenge, Commitment, Feedback, and Task Complexity allowance.

- **SMART goals dominate personal productivity** but were never designed for organizational alignment or multi-stakeholder contexts; they work best for individual, tactical, short-term objectives (under 90 days) [^13^][^21^].

- **OKRs fill the organizational gap** by separating Objectives (qualitative direction) from Key Results (quantitative measures), creating alignment across hierarchy levels. They are intentionally aspirational - 60-80% attainment is considered strong [^21^].

- **HARD goals** (Heartfelt, Animated, Required, Difficult) optimize for emotional engagement rather than measurement, making them superior for long-term personal transformation where motivation is the bottleneck [^14^].

- **PACT goals** (Purposeful, Actionable, Continuous, Trackable) focus on *output* (the journey) rather than *outcome* (the destination), making them ideal for long-term growth, habit formation, and neurodivergent users who find SMART rigid [^18^][^192^].

- **WOOP** (Wish, Outcome, Obstacle, Plan) nearly tripled study time in randomized trials (4.3h vs 1.5h) by combining mental contrasting with implementation intentions [^114^]. Meta-analysis of 24 studies: g = 0.34 effect on goal attainment [^114^].

- **Implementation intentions** ("If X, then Y" plans) produce a medium-to-large effect on goal achievement (d = 0.65) across 94 studies [^15^].

- **BJ Fogg's MAP model** states behavior happens only when Motivation, Ability, and Prompt converge. His key insight: *shrink behaviors to under 30 seconds* to remove ability barriers rather than relying on motivation [^15^].

- **Habit stacking** (linking new habits to existing automatic ones) uses behavior-based cues that are more reliable than time-based cues because they don't require checking the clock [^63^][^68^].

- **Temptation bundling** (pairing "should" behaviors with "want" rewards) increased gym visits by 51% in Milkman's original study, with effects persisting 17 weeks post-intervention [^70^].

- **The 12 Week Year** compresses planning horizons to create urgency, with an execution score (weekly tactic completion) as the leading indicator. Moran recommends 85%+ weekly completion threshold [^89^][^91^].

- **Backward Design** (Wiggins & McTighe) provides the optimal template for goal decomposition: start with the desired result, determine acceptable evidence, then plan the experience [^115^][^117^].

- **Mental health goal patterns** have the strongest evidence base: CBT behavioral activation uses activity monitoring (0-10 mood ratings), pleasure/mastery scheduling, and graded exposure hierarchies (SUDS ratings 0-100) [^145^][^151^][^175^].

- **Fitness periodization** follows structured mesocycles (4-6 weeks) with built-in deload weeks (30-60% volume reduction, 5-20% intensity reduction every 4-8 weeks) [^62^][^64^].

- **Goal taxonomy research** converges on a "Big 4" taxonomy of life meaning dimensions: WIST (Work, Intimacy, Spirituality, Transcendence) [^71^][^195^].

- **2024-2025 app innovations** center on AI-powered pattern recognition, adaptive goal adjustment, emotional-mood correlation tracking, and gamification with RPG elements [^144^][^154^][^197^].

### Goal Taxonomy Comparison

| Taxonomy | Domains Covered | Strengths | Weaknesses | Best For |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **WIST** (Emmons) | Work, Intimacy, Spirituality, Transcendence | Research-backed, captures existential dimensions, predicts well-being | Broad categories, misses health/finance/leisure | Life meaning assessment, holistic coaching |
| **Pinquart et al.** | Health, Psychological, Social, Achievement, Leisure | Empirically validated across cultures, used in rehabilitation research | Healthcare-centric, less applicable to financial/creative goals | Healthcare apps, rehabilitation planning |
| **Emmons Personal Strivings** | Achievement, Intimacy, Religion/Spirituality, Generativity | Captures *why* behind goals, links to virtue and meaning | Abstract, hard to quantify | Deep coaching, therapeutic contexts |
| **Wong's Personal Meaning Profile** | Achievement, Relationship, Religion, Self-transcendence | Integrates Eastern/Western perspectives | Less tested in Western populations | Cross-cultural applications |
| **Big 4 Life Meaning** (Emmons/Ebersole/Wong convergence) | Life narratives, Life work, Relationships, Religious beliefs, Service | Consensual validation across researchers | Overlapping categories | Academic research instrument design |
| **Locke & Latham Task Classification** | Simple vs. Complex tasks (component, coordinative, dynamic complexity) | Explains *when* goals help vs. harm; distinguishes learning vs. performance goals | Doesn't classify by life domain | Determining appropriate goal difficulty and type |

**For ARISE**: The most comprehensive pragmatic taxonomy combines **WIST + Health + Financial + Learning/Creativity + Leisure**, organized into ~10 top-level domains: Health, Fitness, Career/Work, Finance, Relationships, Learning, Creativity, Mental Wellbeing, Spirituality, Environment, and a Custom domain for user-defined categories.

### Framework Comparison: When to Use Each

| Framework | Best For | Bottleneck Addressed | Cadence | Structure Level |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **SMART** | Personal tactical goals, performance reviews, project milestones | Vague, unmeasurable intentions | Days to 90 days | Individual |
| **OKRs** | Team/organizational alignment, strategic execution | Misalignment, lack of transparency | Quarterly/Annual | Organization |
| **HARD** | Long-term aspirations, personal transformation | Motivation collapse, emotional disconnection | 6+ months | Individual |
| **PACT** | Habit building, continuous growth, neurodivergent users | Rigidity, fear of failure, paralysis by analysis | Ongoing | Individual |
| **WOOP (MCII)** | Behavioral follow-through, overcoming procrastination | Knowing what to do but not doing it | 1-12 weeks | Individual |
| **Backward Design** | Learning goals, curriculum design, skill acquisition | Activity without clear outcome | Course/module | Educational |
| **12 Week Year** | Execution-focused professionals, entrepreneurs | Annualized procrastination, low urgency | 12-week cycles | Individual/Small team |
| **Atomic Habits** | Habit ecosystem design, identity transformation | Willpower dependence, all-or-nothing thinking | Daily/continuous | Individual |

**Layering Strategy for ARISE**: Users should be able to choose their primary framework based on goal type:
- **Execution goals** (fitness, finance, career): SMART structure with weekly cadence
- **Transformation goals** (identity, long-term change): HARD emotional layer + SMART tracking layer
- **Habit goals**: PACT or Atomic Habits (identity-based) approach
- **Behavioral goals** (where follow-through fails): WOOP protocol built in
- **Learning goals**: Backward Design (start with assessment/competency)
- **Organizational goals**: OKR alignment

### Decomposition Patterns by Goal Type

#### 1. Fitness Goals
**Template**: Backward Design from competition/event or target metric

| Level | Example: "Run a Marathon" | Example: "Build Strength" |
|---|---|---|
| **Vision (5-year)** | Complete all World Marathon Majors | Deadlift 2.5x bodyweight |
| **Outcome (12-week)** | Finish training block at 40mpw | Complete 12-week strength cycle |
| **Mesocycle (4-week)** | Build base: 25-30mpw | Hypertrophy block: 4x8-12 |
| **Microcycle (1-week)** | 4 runs: 2 easy, 1 tempo, 1 long | 4 sessions: Upper/Lower/Upper/Lower |
| **Daily** | Today's workout: 6mi easy run | Today's workout: Squat 4x8 @ RPE 8 |
| **Progression model** | Weekly mileage increase 10% | Double progression (add reps, then weight) |
| **Deload** | Every 4th week: 50% volume | Every 4th week: 50% sets, -15% weight |

**Key Patterns**:
- **Linear periodization**: Beginners add weight every session
- **Double progression**: Intermediates work in rep ranges, add weight only when topping out
- **RPE/RIR autoregulation**: Advanced lifters adjust daily based on readiness [^62^][^69^]
- **Deload weeks are non-negotiable**: Cut volume 30-60%, reduce intensity 5-20%, maintain movement patterns [^64^]

#### 2. Financial Goals
**Template**: Bucket-based waterfall with milestone gating

| Level | Example: "Financial Independence" |
|---|---|
| **Vision** | Achieve financial independence by 50 |
| **Milestone 1** | Build $1,000 mini-emergency fund |
| **Milestone 2** | Pay off all high-interest debt (snowball/avalanche) |
| **Milestone 3** | Build 3-6 month full emergency fund |
| **Milestone 4** | Save 15-20% of income for retirement |
| **Milestone 5** | Max out tax-advantaged accounts (401k, IRA) |
| **Milestone 6** | Invest in taxable accounts |
| **Daily/Weekly** | Track spending, automate savings transfers, budget review |

**Method Comparison**:
- **Debt Snowball**: Pay smallest balance first → motivation through quick wins [^148^]
- **Debt Avalanche**: Pay highest interest first → mathematically optimal [^149^]
- **50/30/20 Rule**: 50% needs, 30% wants, 20% savings [^95^]
- **Envelope Method**: Cash in categorized envelopes, spending stops when empty [^95^]
- **Pay Yourself First**: Automate savings transfer on payday [^95^]

#### 3. Learning Goals
**Template**: Backward Design from assessment evidence

| Stage | Action | Example: "Learn Python" |
|---|---|---|
| **Identify desired results** | Define competency standard | "Build a working web scraper with error handling" |
| **Determine evidence** | How will you prove competence? | "Submit functional project to GitHub" |
| **Plan learning experiences** | Curriculum and practice structure | "Complete course → practice problems → build project" |
| **Deliberate practice** | Focused skill work on weak areas | "Spend 20 min daily on regex exercises" |
| **Spaced repetition** | Retention scheduling | "Review flashcards every day, algorithm determines intervals" |
| **Comprehension check** | Self-testing milestones | "Explain concept to rubber duck (Feynman Technique)" |
| **Bloom's progression** | Cognitive level targets | Remember → Understand → Apply → Analyze → Evaluate → Create |

**Key Patterns**:
- **Deliberate practice**: Focus on specific weaknesses with immediate feedback, not just repetition [^98^]
- **Spaced repetition**: Review at increasing intervals (1 day → 3 days → 7 days → 21 days) [^98^]
- **Feynman Technique**: Explain concept in simple terms; gaps in explanation reveal gaps in understanding [^98^]
- **Interleaved practice**: Mix different problem types rather than blocking by topic [^98^]
- **Project-based milestones**: Build something real at each competency level

#### 4. Mental Health Goals
**Template**: CBT Behavioral Activation + Graded Exposure

**Pattern A: Behavioral Activation (for depression/anhedonia)**
| Stage | Action | Metrics |
|---|---|---|
| **Activity monitoring** | Track daily activities + mood ratings | Mood 0-10, Pleasure 0-10, Mastery 0-10 [^151^] |
| **Pattern identification** | Identify "up activities" and "down activities" | Weekly review of activity-mood correlations |
| **Values clarification** | Define what matters in key life domains | Life domains worksheet |
| **Activity scheduling** | Plan pleasure + mastery activities weekly | Weekly planner with scheduled activities [^145^] |
| **Behavioral experiments** | Test predictions: "If I do X, my mood will be Y" | Predicted vs. actual mood ratings [^151^] |
| **Graded increase** | Start micro (5 min), build gradually | Daily completion tracking |

**Pattern B: Exposure Hierarchy (for anxiety/phobias)**
| Stage | Action | Metrics |
|---|---|---|
| **Define fear family** | One theme per hierarchy (e.g., social situations) | SUDS: Subjective Units of Distress Scale (0-100) |
| **Brain-dump situations** | List 10-15 feared scenarios | Don't censor, range helps |
| **Rate SUDS** | Estimate distress for each scenario | 0 = no distress, 100 = maximum [^175^] |
| **Sort ladder** | Order from easiest to hardest | Lowest SUDS first |
| **Pick start point** | Choose item rated ≤ 40 SUDS | Build confidence before advancing |
| **Plan exposure** | What, where, when, for how long | Calendar specificity beats intentions |
| **Run & record** | Pre-SUDS → exposure → post-SUDS | Stay until distress drops ≥ 50% or 30 min |
| **Repeat & step up** | Repeat until two sessions end ≤ 20 SUDS | Then advance to next rung [^177^][^180^] |

#### 5. Relationship Goals
**Template**: Habit-based micro-interactions

| Level | Example: "Be a Better Partner" |
|---|---|
| **Identity shift** | "I am a thoughtful partner" (not "I want to be...") |
| **Daily micro-action** | One intentional act of appreciation daily |
| **Weekly ritual** | Dedicated connection time (date night, check-in) |
| **Communication practice** | Active listening exercise 3x/week |
| **Monthly review** | Relationship satisfaction rating 1-10 |
| **Quarterly goal** | Address one growth area identified by partner |

#### 6. Career Goals
**Template**: OKR-style with skill stack milestones

| Level | Example: "Become a Senior Engineer" |
|---|---|
| **Objective** | Earn promotion to Senior Engineer |
| **Key Result 1** | Lead 2 cross-functional projects to completion |
| **Key Result 2** | Mentor 2 junior developers to independent contribution |
| **Key Result 3** | Complete system design certification |
| **Skill acquisition** | Deliberate practice in targeted competency each month |
| **Weekly actions** | One networking conversation, one skill-building activity |
| **Daily habits** | 30 min technical reading, code review participation |

### Measurement Proxy Strategies

#### Why Abstract Goals Fail
Vague goals like "be more present" or "be happier" fail Locke's Clarity principle. They cannot be measured, so they cannot be tracked, so they cannot be achieved [^181^]. The solution is **proxy indicators** - measurable behaviors that correlate with the abstract state.

| Abstract Goal | Measurable Proxy | Rationale | Tracking Method |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Be more present/mindful" | Log 3 intentional mindful moments per day | Presence is a state; intentional moments are a behavior | Daily tally, app notification check-ins |
| "Be happier" | Rate daily mood 0-10; track "up activities" | Subjective well-being is measurable with repetition; behavioral activation research shows activity-mood links [^151^] | Mood journal, activity correlation |
| "Reduce anxiety" | Complete 2 exposure hierarchy items per week; track peak SUDS | Anxiety reduction is tracked through behavioral engagement and distress tolerance [^175^] | Exposure log, pre/post SUDS ratings |
| "Be more grateful" | Write 3 specific gratitudes daily | Gratitude journaling is the most validated intervention [^122^] | Daily journal entries |
| "Be a better friend" | Initiate 1 meaningful conversation per week; remember 1 important date | Relationship quality correlates with initiation and remembering | Calendar tracking, contact log |
| "Find purpose" | Complete values clarification exercise; align weekly activities with top 3 values | Purpose is expressed through values-congruent action | Weekly values-alignment score |
| "Be more confident" | Record 1 "confidence vote" (evidence of capability) daily; track comfort-zone expansions | Confidence builds through accumulated evidence | Daily log, exposure tracker |
| "Improve discipline" | Track daily habit streak for 2 core habits; calculate weekly execution percentage | Discipline is a meta-skill expressed through consistency | Habit tracker, execution scorecard |
| "Reduce stress" | Practice 1 relaxation technique daily; track stress triggers and coping efficacy | Stress management is behavioral; efficacy is measurable | Daily stress rating 0-10, coping log |
| "Be more creative" | Complete 15-min creative practice daily; ship 1 creative work per month | Creativity is a practice, not a trait; output is measurable | Daily practice log, monthly shipping calendar |

#### Well-Being Measurement Frameworks
The World Happiness Foundation proposes five measurable dimensions of "happy people" [^147^]:
1. **Self-Awareness & Inner Peace**: MAAS (Mindful Attention Awareness Scale), WHO-5 Well-Being Index
2. **Shadow Integration & Emotional Regulation**: DERS (Difficulties in Emotion Regulation Scale), PANAS (Positive and Negative Affect Schedule)
3. **Compassion & Interbeing**: Self-Compassion Scale, Watts Connectedness Scale
4. **Purpose & Meaning-Making**: MLQ (Meaning in Life Questionnaire)

### Habit Formation Science: Complete Toolkit

#### BJ Fogg's MAP Model (Tiny Habits)
**Core formula**: Behavior = Motivation + Ability + Prompt (all three must converge)

| Element | Strategy | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Motivation** | Minimize dependence; spike motivation with celebration | Do a celebration immediately after the tiny behavior |
| **Ability** | **Make it TINY** - under 30 seconds, under 2 minutes | "Floss 1 tooth" not "floss all teeth" |
| **Prompt** | Anchor to existing automatic behavior | "After I pour coffee, I will..." |

**Key insight**: "Emotions create habits, not repetition." Immediate celebration wires the behavior faster than repetition alone [^15^].

#### James Clear's Atomic Habits (Four Laws)
| Law | For Good Habits | For Breaking Bad Habits |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Cue** | Make it obvious | Make it invisible |
| **2. Craving** | Make it attractive | Make it unattractive |
| **3. Response** | Make it easy | Make it difficult |
| **4. Reward** | Make it satisfying | Make it unsatisfying |

**Identity-based habits**: "The goal is not to run a marathon, the goal is to become a runner." Every action is a vote for your desired identity [^188^][^193^].

#### Habit Stacking Formula
"After I [CURRENT HABIT], I will [NEW HABIT]." [^63^][^68^]
- Uses behavior-based cues (more reliable than time-based)
- Current habit must be truly automatic
- New habit must start under 2 minutes

#### Temptation Bundling Formula
"While I [HABIT I NEED], I will [HABIT I WANT]." [^70^]
- Only allowed to do the "want" while doing the "should"
- Works by countering present bias with immediate reward
- 51% increase in gym visits in original study [^70^]

#### Implementation Intentions
"If [situation X], then I will [behavior Y]." [^15^]
- Medium-to-large effect: d = 0.65 across 94 studies
- "If" clause must be a real-world cue you'll actually notice
- "Then" clause must be a single observable action

#### Automaticity Curve (Lally et al.)
- Habits form on an asymptotic curve
- Biggest gains in first few weeks
- Range: 18-254 days to reach automaticity (average ~66 days)
- Missing once doesn't break the streak; consistency matters most

### Time-Based Goal Structuring

#### The 12 Week Year (Moran & Lennington)
| Element | Description | Key Insight |
|---|---|---|
| **Cycle length** | 12 weeks execution + 1 week transition/review | Creates urgency that annual plans lack [^89^] |
| **Goals per cycle** | 2-4 maximum | Too many goals dilute execution [^93^] |
| **Weekly plan** | Lead indicators (controllable inputs that drive lag results) | Focus on behaviors, not outcomes |
| **Execution score** | % of planned tactics completed weekly | Target 85%+ for strong performance [^89^] |
| **Weekly review** | 20-30 min at fixed time; score execution, adjust tactics | Consistency beats intensity |
| **Why it works** | Compresses delay-discount factor; weekly feedback loop | Proximate deadlines create effort [^91^] |

#### Comparison of Cadences
| Cadence | Framework | Best For | Review Frequency |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Daily** | Habit tracking, execution scoring | Behavioral consistency | Daily (evening) |
| **Weekly** | 12 Week Year tactics, habit review | Maintaining momentum | Weekly (fixed time) |
| **Bi-weekly** | Sprint-based work | Rapid iteration | Every 2 weeks |
| **Monthly** | SMART personal goals | Mid-term course correction | Month-end |
| **Quarterly** | OKRs, 12 Week Year cycles | Strategic alignment | Week 13 review |
| **Annual** | Vision setting, life planning | Long-term direction | Year-end reflection |

#### Integration for ARISE
The app should support **nested timeframes**:
- **Daily**: Habit completion, mood ratings, task execution
- **Weekly**: Execution score review, tactic adjustment
- **Monthly**: Progress assessment, milestone check
- **Quarterly**: Goal cycle review, new 12-week plan
- **Annual**: Vision alignment, life domain assessment

### Goal Conflict Resolution

#### Common Goal Conflicts
| Conflict | Type | Resolution Strategy |
|---|---|---|
| **Cut calories vs. build muscle** | Physiological | Alternating phases: cut → maintenance → bulk; protein priority during cuts |
| **Save money vs. invest in career** | Resource allocation | Calculate ROI of career investments; time-box experimental spending |
| **Work late vs. family time** | Time scarcity | Define "non-negotiable" time blocks; use boundaries, not balance |
| **Many goals, limited time** | Attention scarcity | Limit active goals to 2-4 per cycle (12 Week Year rule) [^93^] |
| **Short-term comfort vs. long-term health** | Present bias | WOOP mental contrasting; temptation bundling; identity shift |
| **Learning vs. doing** | Exploration-exploitation | Time-block both; 80% doing, 20% learning |
| **Consistency vs. intensity** | Sustainability | PACT continuous approach; deload weeks; "never miss twice" rule |

#### Resolution Frameworks
1. **Eisenhower Matrix**: Urgent/Important classification for prioritization [^97^]
2. **Assumption Mapping**: Test underlying hypotheses of competing priorities [^97^]
3. **North Star Definition**: Identify the single priority that drives all decisions [^97^]
4. **Dependency Analysis**: Map which goals enable others; prioritize enablers [^97^]
5. **Opportunity Solution Tree** (Torres): Visualize solutions against opportunities to identify conflicts early [^96^]

### Appendix: Full Framework Details

#### Locke & Latham's Five Principles (Complete)
| Principle | Requirement | What Undermines It | ARISE Implementation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Clarity** | Specific, measurable target describable in one sentence | "Do your best," vague KPIs, changing targets | Force measurability at goal creation; proxy indicators for abstract goals |
| **Challenge** | Above current performance but within achievable range | Too easy (no stretch) or impossible (no buy-in) | Calibrate to current + 10-30%; AI difficulty adjustment |
| **Commitment** | Person genuinely accepts the goal, ideally co-set | Top-down imposition without participation | User-chosen goals with ownership rituals |
| **Feedback** | Visible, near-real-time progress data | Annual reviews only, delayed feedback | Weekly execution scores, daily habit streaks, real-time progress bars |
| **Task Complexity** | Time and learning runway proportional to difficulty | Same deadline regardless of skill level | Decompose complex goals; learning goals for novices, performance goals for experts |

#### WOOP Protocol (Step-by-Step for App Integration)
1. **Wish**: 3-6 words, 1-4 week horizon, challenging but feasible
2. **Outcome**: Vividly imagine best result for 60-90 seconds (bounded to prevent demobilization)
3. **Obstacle**: Name the *inner* obstacle (behavior or feeling, not external circumstance). Must be specific and slightly uncomfortable to admit.
4. **Plan**: Single if-then sentence. "If [specific trigger], then I will [single action]."

**Daily cadence**: Full protocol once at goal creation, then mentally walk through the if-then plan once daily.

**When WOOP fails**: Obstacle is too vague; trigger isn't a real-world cue; "then" clause is too complex; skipping the obstacle step (collapses to wishful thinking) [^114^].
